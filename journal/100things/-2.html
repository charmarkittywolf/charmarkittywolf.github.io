I'm Gonna Do 100 Things!

March 2nd, 2026

On February 15, 2026, Kristian sent me this article.

I didn't really think that much of it at the time. (I'm not even sure I read it other than skimming it a bit.)

But it seems to have stuck with me, because 2 months later while thinking about how to live a more enriching life I instantly and without any hesitation knew that it was what I should be doing.

I am going to do 100 Things.

And I am going to do 100 Things not for the sake of having completed 100 different things. But for the sake of experiencing pursuing those 100 different things.

The journey is more important than the destination. But in order to have a journey one must still have a destination.

I have a “purpose”, “minimum achievement”, and “goal” for each thing. My thought process as to why to include these considerations is below.

Purpose

I feel like I shouldn't really need a purpose other than 'feeling like doing it' for any task on the list, but I have two different reasons for requiring myself to outline one anyway:

  1. This list is partially the result of a desire to hold myself accountable in my pursuits. Outlining a purpose helps keep me centered on what it even is I'm looking to get out of any given task so that I can craft my expectations of failure and success around it.

  2. The complexities of posting anything on the fake hell-layer of reality that is the internet demands that I know what I'm aiming to get out of any endeavor. If I'm making something to engage with the craft, that's one thing. I shouldn't even need to post those types of creations at all, frankly. But. If I have aspirations for success I need to take those seriously and not just pretend I don't care because I don't wanna get upset when I fail.

    My self-preservational instinct is to say “Oh I'm just doing this for me.” when making art. But let's be real, if I'm intending to post it online, some part of me seeks and craves attention and success, and at that point I should shoot for success itself with everything I have, rather than protect my ego by pretending I'm some enlightened artist who's doing it purely for me.

    Furthermore, part of my process for creation is to deliberately craft an experience for the audience, and in order to do that there fundamentally must be an audience in mind. And so outlining that intended audience as part of my purpose becomes important. If my desire is to find success, I must unfortunately embrace that and find ways to self express that's also tailored towards an unknown phantom audience of fake internet people. ...what a nightmare.

Minimum Acheivement

But if I'm shooting for “success” rather than just “making a thing” then I need to make sure I have an emergency exit. I'm not always gonna find success, and I need to be prepared for that outcome. …expect it even. But I also want to give it 100% before bowing out, and not simply stop due to discouragement. Which is why, for some of my more ambitious endeavors on this list, I needed a “Failure Point”. An outlined point in which I can tell myself I genuinely tried and its okay to stop now. Most of the time, this is simply creating a certain number of creations in my pursuit of “success”. Note: I do not HAVE to stop once I reach the failure point. I'm simply allowed to at that point.

Goal

But what even is “success”? I need to decide at what point I can satisfactorily say I achieved my goal. And perhaps at that point I can keep going if it's fulfilling enough of a pursuit, but that’s something to decide at that point. I try to do research to understand what is a reasonable “above average” sort of remarkable metric for any given pursuit, and keep it in that range.

With all of that figured out, let's get to the first task!

Task (1): Make A Website!
